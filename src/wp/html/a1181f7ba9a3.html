This liver disease is caused by the hepatitis A virus. It differs from other types of hepatitis, such as hepatitis B or C.<\/p>\n

Hepatitis A is most often transmitted by eating food or drinking water containing the hepatitis A virus. It is most often transmitted when traveling in parts of the world where hepatitis A is more common than in Canada. Contaminated sources may include :<\/p>\n

the ice
\nraw or undercooked shellfish
\nraw or frozen fruits and vegetables (peel them yourself to reduce the risk)
\nHepatitis A can also be transmitted :<\/p>\n

    \n
  • eating food prepared by an infected person<\/li>\n
  • from one person to another (rarely):\n
      \n
    • contact with the stools of an infected person<\/li>\n
    • through sexual contact with an infected person<\/li>\n
    • changing the diaper or cleaning the stool of an infected person<\/li>\n
    • through blood transfusions or the sharing of needles for injecting drugs<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n

      Even if you have no symptoms, you can infect other people. Infected children and infants often have no symptoms, unlike infected adults.<\/p>\n

      The virus can be transmitted up to two weeks before the onset of symptoms. You can continue to infect others for about a week after the onset of jaundice (yellowing of the skin and whites of the eyes).<\/p>\n
