Japanese encephalitis is caused by a Flavivirus.<\/p>\n

Japanese encephalitis is found in almost all Asian countries and parts of the Western Pacific. It poses a threat mainly in rural agricultural regions, particularly in areas where irrigation (artificial watering of crops) is widespread, including rice paddies. It can take from 5 to 15 days for symptoms to appear. Most cases are asymptomatic.<\/p>\n

Less than 1% of those infected develop encephalitis (swelling of the brain), which is fatal in 20-30% of cases.<\/p>\n

Severe cases are usually characterized by a sudden onset of :<\/p>\n

    \n
  • fever<\/li>\n
  • headaches<\/li>\n
  • vomiting<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n

    Other symptoms include:<\/p>\n

      \n
    • a stiff neck<\/li>\n
    • mental confusion<\/li>\n
    • mental or behavioural changes<\/li>\n
    • general weakness<\/li>\n
    • motor disorders<\/li>\n
    • paralysis (inability to move)<\/li>\n
    • a coma<\/li>\n
    • convulsions<\/li>\n
    • nerve and brain damage that may be permanent<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n
