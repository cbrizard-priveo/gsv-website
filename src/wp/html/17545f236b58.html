Travel outside Canada<\/strong><\/h2>

Measles prevention is particularly important if you’re planning to travel. You may need additional doses of measles vaccine before traveling outside Canada.<\/p>

If you plan to travel outside Canada :<\/p>

  • consult the global measles advisory<\/a><\/li>
  • make sure your basic vaccination record is up to date<\/a><\/li>
  • read the travel advice and warnings for your destination<\/a><\/li>
  • consult a health care provider or make an appointment at a travel health clinic at least 6 weeks before departure<\/li><\/ul>

    <\/p>

    Make sure you’ve had at least 1 dose of a measles vaccine if you have no history of measles and were born before 1970.<\/p>

    2 doses of measles vaccine are recommended for :<\/p>

    • born in 1970 or later<\/li>
    • aged 12 months and over<\/li>
    • who have no history of measles<\/li><\/ul>

      <\/p>

      Traveling infants can be vaccinated against measles, mumps and rubella (MMR) from the age of 6 months. They should receive 1 dose before traveling if they are under 1 year old.<\/p>

      The best way to determine whether you are fully protected against measles or need additional vaccinations is to consult a health care provider.<\/p>

      Consult a health-care provider if you develop measles symptoms while travelling or after returning to Canada. Call ahead so precautions can be taken to help prevent spread to others.<\/p>

      If you notice any symptoms of measles during your flight, inform :<\/p>

      • flight crew before landing<\/li>
      • the border services officer when you enter the country<\/li><\/ul>

        <\/p>

        A quarantine officer will assess your symptoms and provide assistance.<\/p>
